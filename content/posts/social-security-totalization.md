## Introduction

Moving between the U.S. and Canada is a common career path, but it often leaves workers worried about their retirement. A major concern for many is the "10-year rule"—the requirement to have 40 work credits (roughly 10 years of work) to qualify for U.S. Social Security.

If you worked in the U.S. for only five or six years before moving to Canada, you might assume those tax contributions are "lost." Fortunately, the **Canada-U.S. Social Security Totalization Agreement** exists specifically to prevent this.

---

## Bridging the Gap: The Canada-U.S. Social Security Agreement

The Totalization Agreement is a bilateral treaty designed to coordinate the pension programs of both countries. It serves two primary purposes:

1. **Eliminating Double Taxation:** Ensuring you don't pay into both systems for the same income.
2. **Protecting Benefit Eligibility:** Allowing you to combine your periods of work in both countries to meet the minimum requirements for a pension.

### The "6-Credit" Rule

While the U.S. usually requires 40 credits to qualify for retirement benefits, the agreement allows the Social Security Administration (SSA) to look at your Canadian work history to help you cross that finish line.

To use this agreement, you must meet one critical baseline: **You must have earned at least 6 U.S. credits (approximately 1.5 years of work) under the U.S. system.**

If you have at least 6 credits but fewer than 40, the SSA will "totalize" your record by counting your years of contribution to the **Canada Pension Plan (CPP)** or **Quebec Pension Plan (QPP)**.

### How the Calculation Works

It is important to understand that while Canadian credits help you **qualify** for a payment, they do not **increase** the amount of that payment.

* **Eligibility:** The U.S. uses your Canadian time to see if you are *eligible* for a check.
* **Payment Amount:** Once eligibility is established, the U.S. calculates your actual monthly benefit based **only** on your U.S. earnings. You will receive a "pro-rated" or partial U.S. benefit.

---

## What About Canadian Benefits?

The agreement works both ways. If you lived or worked in the U.S. but don't have enough years to qualify for the **Old Age Security (OAS)** or **CPP** in Canada:

* **OAS:** Usually requires 10 years of residence in Canada after age 18. Under the agreement, your years of residence in the U.S. can count toward this 10-year requirement.
* **CPP:** Since you only need one valid contribution to qualify for a CPP retirement pension, the totalization agreement is more commonly used to qualify for CPP **disability** or **survivor** benefits.

---

## How to Apply

You do not need to apply twice. You generally apply for benefits in the country where you currently reside:

1. **If you live in Canada:** Apply through **Service Canada**. They will provide you with the necessary forms (such as Form **CDN-USA 1** or **ISP-5005-USA**) and will coordinate with the U.S. Social Security Administration on your behalf.
2. **If you live in the U.S.:** Apply through your local **SSA office**. They will request your Canadian record to complete the totalization process.

### Summary Table: Qualifying for U.S. Benefits

| Requirement | Standard Rule | Under Totalization Agreement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Minimum U.S. Credits** | 40 Credits (10 years) | 6 Credits (1.5 years) |
| **Combining Credits** | Not allowed | Use CPP/QPP years to reach 40 credits |
| **Benefit Amount** | Based on full U.S. history | Pro-rated based only on U.S. earnings |
